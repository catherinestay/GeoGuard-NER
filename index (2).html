Early warning from inside the hillside.

GeoGuard-NER is a network of solar-powered sensor anchors for unstable slopes in North-East India. Each node reads soil moisture, pore water pressure, rainfall and tilt, then warns highway authorities before the slope fails, even when monsoon cloud blinds the satellites and the phone network drops.

01 The problem

When the monsoon closes in, the slope goes dark.

Landslides and debris flows cut NH-27 and NH-44, trap supply chains and kill people. The usual tools fail in exactly the weather that triggers them.

Satellites cannot see the slope

Optical and radar satellites lose the ground under dense monsoon cloud across North-East India. GeoGuard-NER measures movement and water below the surface instead.

Phones do not reach the highway

Long highway stretches have no cellular coverage. Nodes pass readings from one to the next over a LoRaWAN mesh until one reaches a gateway.

Alerts arrive after the movement

A warning that waits for someone to read a dashboard comes late. GeoGuard-NER alerts the District Disaster Management Authority and triggers road-gate sirens directly.

02 How it works

A packet's journey, from rebar to road gate.

  1. Sense

    Slope node

    An anchored node captures soil moisture, pore water pressure, rainfall intensity and tilt, around the clock on solar power.

  2. Relay

    LoRaWAN mesh

    Packets re-route around mountain obstacles, hop by hop, to the nearest gateway.

  3. Split

    Gateway

    The gateway branches in two: an offline siren and strobe, and the cloud and AI core.

  4. Predict

    AI core

    Readings flow through MQTT into InfluxDB. A 72-hour LSTM model, checked against slope physics, estimates the Factor of Safety.

  5. Act

    Dashboard and alerts

    A live GIS dashboard shows Normal, Watch or Evacuate. DDMA alerts and road-gate sirens fire from the same signal.

Physics checks the AI

The LSTM does not decide alone. Its forecast is held against the classical infinite-slope stability equation, which cuts false alarms from rain noise and passing machinery. Kalman filtering removes high-frequency vibration before any threshold is tested.

FoS = [ c′ + (γ − m·γw) · z · cos²β · tan φ′ ] / [ γ · z · sin β · cos β ] c′ cohesion · γ soil unit weight · γw water unit weight · m saturated fraction of the soil layer · z depth to slip plane · β slope angle · φ′ friction angle. FoS below 1.0 means the slope fails.
FoS 1.30 and above

Normal

Slope is stable. Nodes report on the routine schedule and the dashboard shows green.

FoS 1.10 to 1.30

Watch

Margin is shrinking as the soil saturates. Authorities are notified to prepare and monitor closely.

FoS below 1.10

Evacuate

Failure is close. The DDMA is alerted and the road-gate siren and strobe trigger at the gateway, even offline.

04 Node hardware

Anatomy of a node.

Built from locally sourced parts and anchored in the slope.

ASolar and power
Monocrystalline panel, TP4056 charge controller and LiFePO4 battery. Deep-sleep firmware targets 14 sunless monsoon days.
BLoRa radio
SX1276 module on a LoRaWAN mesh with multi-hop re-routing.
CController
ESP32-S3 in an IP67 weatherproof enclosure, set in concrete.
DTilt sensor
MPU6050 accelerometer and gyroscope for tilt and vibration, with Kalman filtering.
EGround anchor
Iron rebar anchor retrofitted into the unstable slope.
FSubsurface probe
Soil moisture and pore water pressure sensing below ground.
GRain gauge
Rainfall intensity at the node.

What can go wrong, and the planned answer

Mountain canopy and terrain shadowingSteep valleys attenuate and block LoRa signals.
Multi-hop mesh routingPackets re-route around obstacles to the nearest gateway.
Noise and false alarmsHeavy rain impact, soil compaction and passing machinery cause vibration spikes.
Kalman filtering and sensor fusionNoise is removed before alert thresholds are tested.
Tampering and rockfallRemote nodes face vandalism and surface damage.
Concrete-anchored IP67 enclosuresJunction boxes set in concrete, held by reinforced steel rebar.
